Can AI outgrow the planet’s power grid?

An interactive series · switches, chips, learning, scaling, power and orbit · by Akarsh Gopal, November 2026

AI’s electricity is still small, about 55 TWh of inference in 2025 by this series’ estimate, a fifth of one per cent of the world’s. The money behind it is not: four companies plan to spend about $730 billion on data centres and chips in 2026, enough for about 19 GW of AI capacity in a year.1 This series asks whether the grid can connect what that money is buying this decade, and keeps the long-run question, whether AI becomes humanity’s largest use of energy, as a bookend.

Start with the essay. It is the whole argument in one sitting, from a switch you can tap to the turbines and transformers that now set the pace. The pages below are its deep dives, each written to be read alone; the map at the end shows how they fit.

2The case, as a map

Each card is a page that explains one condition for, or one force against, the thesis. Every card is a live link; cards you have opened in this browser are tinted, and the pips show which way you have set that page’s slider on the long-run meter.

What this series is not. Not a forecast. The 55 TWh is derived, from the IEA’s data-centre total, a year’s growth and a 60% inference share taken from a secondary source; its band is 40–80 TWh, and the token counts companies publish explain only 3 to 8 TWh of it. Where something is an assumption, the figure’s ⓘ data panel says so.
