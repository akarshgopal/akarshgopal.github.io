How Electricity Works

An interactive series in nine essays · by Akarsh Gopal · October 2026

Flip a switch and a lamp lights instantly. Somewhere, hundreds of kilometres away, a machine the size of a house turns very slightly harder. In between are wires that hum at exactly fifty (or, in the Americas and western Japan, sixty) cycles a second, an auction that ran yesterday at noon, a bill built up in layers, and more than a century of engineering, money and politics.

This series follows that chain from one end to the other, in nine essays meant to be read in order. The first three build the machine: how a spinning magnet makes electricity, and how transformers and lines carry it across a country. The fourth keeps it in balance, second by second and hour by hour. The next two turn to the newcomers, solar panels and wind turbines. The last three follow the money: what a bill is made of and what plants cost, what cheap electricity makes possible, and whether it can get cheaper again.

Almost every figure is live. A tag on each caption says whether it plots real data or a simplified model built to show a mechanism.

The essays

  1. 1The Machine: Electrons, Magnets and SteamWhat a current is, how a spinning magnet makes one, three phases, and why most electricity still comes from boiling water.
  2. 2Transformers: The Quiet BottleneckWhy power travels at high voltage, the copper-and-iron box that makes that possible, and why one now takes years to arrive.
  3. 3Transmission: The Network That Decides What Gets BuiltWhat limits a line, how power flows through a meshed network, when direct current wins, and why new lines take a decade.
  4. 4Balancing the Grid: Fifty Hertz and the Markets That Keep ItHow a continent stays at fifty hertz second by second, and how markets choose which plants run each hour and in each place.
  5. 5Solar PV: From Photon to GridFrom a photon in silicon to a panel on a roof or a field, the factories behind the price collapse, and the noon problem.
  6. 6Utility-Scale Renewables: Building and Running the FleetWind turbines from the cube law to wakes, and what it takes to connect, finance and run wind and solar farms.
  7. 7The Price of Electricity: Bills, Plants and Learning CurvesThe layers of a bill, what a power plant costs, why solar got cheaper while nuclear got dearer, and what a clean grid costs.
  8. 8Synthetic Fuels and the Price of a Kilowatt-HourWhat a megawatt-hour becomes in aluminium, steel and hydrogen, and why fuels made from electricity are mostly its price.
  9. 9Cheaper Again? A Century of Prices and the Next Twenty YearsWhy electricity got cheaper every year until 1973 and then stopped, and what would have to be true for it to fall again.
