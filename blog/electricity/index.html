Why Did Electricity Stop Getting Cheaper?

How electricity works, in nine essays · by Akarsh Gopal · October 2026

For eighty years electricity got cheaper. An American household paid about $5 for a kilowatt-hour in 1892, in 2002 dollars, and under 10 cents by 1970. Then the fall stopped. Once inflation is taken out, Americans now pay roughly what they paid in 1970.

Since then the world has learned to make power from sunlight more cheaply than from almost anything else, and yet the American household price has risen by more than a third since 2016, and German households pay more than twice the American price. This series asks why the cheapening stopped and whether it can start again. Along the way it builds everything the answer needs: what a kilowatt-hour is, how a power station makes one, how wires and markets deliver it, and what each part of its price is made of.

It follows four countries, the United States, Germany, China and India, in nine essays meant to be read in order.

The essays

  2. 2What a Kilowatt-Hour Is: Charge, Voltage and HeatCharge and current, voltage as height, resistance as heat, and power versus energy: the rules every later essay obeys.
  3. 3The Machine: Magnets, Steam and the Limits of HeatHow a spinning magnet makes electricity, why most of it still comes from boiling water, and how bigger, hotter plants made it cheap until they hit a ceiling.
  4. 4Wires: The Network That Decides What Gets BuiltWhy power travels at high voltage, what limits a line, why new lines take a decade, and how the network became a growing part of the bill.
  5. 5Balancing the Grid: Fifty Hertz and the Markets That Keep ItHow supply meets demand every second, and how the last plant needed sets the price for all, so that a fuel shock reaches every kilowatt-hour.
  6. 6Solar: The Engine RestartsFrom photon to panel, the learning curve that made solar the cheapest new power, China’s factories and India’s auctions, and the noon problem.
  7. 7Wind and the Fleet: Building It, Running It, and What It Is WorthWind physics, capacity factors, the queue to connect, and why wind and solar power are worth less the more of them there is.
  8. 8What a Kilowatt-Hour Costs: Plants, Capital and the BillWhat a plant costs over its life, why the cost of capital matters so much, why nuclear got dearer, and the bill from exchange to meter in four countries.
  9. 9Why It Stopped, and Can It Fall Again?The pieces put together: why the cheapening ended, why the price of electricity moves everything else, and what would have to be true for it to fall again.
