The Yardstick: How Big Is the Bar?

Can AI outgrow the planet’s power grid? · a deep dive · from a lamp to a planet · written for Akarsh Gopal, October 2026

Hold a 100-watt lamp lit for a little under nine seconds. That is 0.24 Wh — Google’s median Gemini text prompt at the data-centre meter.1 Count prompts from the bottom up and even ten billion a day comes to under 1 TWh/yr. Split the IEA’s data-centre electricity from the top down instead and AI inference lands near 55 TWh/yr (derived; anchor band 40–80): ~0.2% of world electricity.2 The gap of fifty-fold or more says most inference energy is not chatbot text: recommendation and ad ranking, API and agent calls, images and video, chips idling in reserve for peaks, and prompts heavier than the median. This page asks how big the bars are that the thesis has to beat, and how fast those bars move.

It sets the bar slider on the Act 1 meter. Everything else in the series is evidence for or against clearing it.

1Where 55 TWh sits

Primary energy — everything extracted — was 600.3 EJ in 2025 (167,000 TWh).3 Final energy — what end-users burn or draw — is ~450 EJ. Electricity is 21% of final today and ~25% by 2035 under STEPS.4 Data centres: 485 TWh in 2025; AI inference (derived split): 55 TWh (band 40–80).

2From primary to the meter

Primary energy is extracted; final energy is what end-users burn or draw. IEA final-energy shares: industry 38%, transport 28%, buildings 28%; electricity is 21% of final today and ~25% by 2035 (STEPS).4 Data centres and AI are thin next to heating, cooling or transport — thick enough to move national grids.

3Three rungs

Three bars, each with a year:

  • R1 — ≥10% of world electricity by 2035: ≈3,800 TWh from IEA WEO 2025 CPS/STEPS ≈37,800 TWh that year.8
  • R2 — larger than any other single electricity end-use by 2050: working bar 6,000 TWh. Chain: space cooling 2,900 TWh (2025) +1,600 by 2035 → ~4,500; IEA tracking “more than double by 2050 with no action” → ≳5,800, rounded to 6,000.6 Free WEO tables publish no single “largest 2050 end-use” cell. Industrial motor systems (~10,400 TWh, 2023) span many end-uses — not the bar. Count them as one use and R2 would sit near 10,000 TWh already, nearly double; Mid’s NO holds either way.
  • R3 — larger than any other single use of energy by 2050: 17,200 TWh = buildings space-and-water heating in 2021 (62 EJ).5 “Single use” is slippery, so I take the smallest of the big candidates, which is kind to the thesis. Count all road-transport fuel as one use instead and the bar rises: transport is ~28% of ~450 EJ final energy, and road roughly three-quarters of that, ≈95 EJ ≈ 26,000 TWh.4 Mid’s NO holds against either bar.

At Mid defaults the path sits far below every bar. Sensitivity: if the 60% inference share is wrong and the true share is 40%, M0 ≈ 37 TWh — still inside the 40–80 band, and a factor-of-two miss does not flip Mid’s NO on R1–R3. Drag the index meter for the live path.

4The bar moves

Cooling, EVs, heat pumps and industry grow too. Data centres are <10% of world electricity-demand growth through 2030 in the IEA Base Case.7 The index “other uses grow” slider scales the bars: electrifying heat and transport raises R1 and R2, not automatically R3.

For what a terawatt-hour costs, and why the price of electricity moves everything else, see the sister series: Why the price of electricity moves everything else, Solar PV costs, and utility renewables.

Final words

The bar is large, and it is moving. The rest of the case asks whether demand, efficiency and supply can close a gap of two orders of magnitude before 2035 or 2050. Open the index meter and drag “other uses grow”; the bars slide.
