The Demand: Who Asks, How Often?

OpenAI reported 100 million weekly ChatGPT users in November 2023 and 1.2 billion by October 2026 — a 12× rise in three years, before API and agent traffic.1 This page asks what “demand” means for the Act 1 meter: people × tasks × tokens. Company user counts are not independently audited.

It sets the people × tasks and tokens per task sliders. Scaling explained why bigger models and reasoning raise tokens per answer; this page multiplies by who is asking.

1Tokens per task

Google’s median Gemini text prompt is 0.24 Wh at the meter.1b The chat rung’s ~400 tokens is my conversion at that average — Google did not publish a token count for the median. The IEA says reasoning, agents and video can use “hundreds or thousands of times” more energy per task;2 the three upper rungs are order-of-magnitude estimates that scale Wh with the same multiple as tokens. That mapping is the murk: video and tool use break Wh∝tokens. One measured rung; three labelled estimates. Sliders = the meter’s U and T.

Why watt-hours are not tokens

The meter’s T lever is “tokens per task vs today.” The ladder’s upper rungs set T from a token-count multiple of the chat rung, then display Wh as 0.24 × that multiple. That is a labelled approximation, not a measurement: a reasoning pass can burn more FLOP per token (search, self-check); a short video is billed in frames and diffusion steps, not tokens; tool-using agents spend energy on the tools. When the IEA says “hundreds or thousands of times” more energy per task, it is talking about Wh, not tokens. Use the ladder to set order of magnitude; treat the Wh column on estimate rungs as a sketch.

2When cheaper means more

Google reported a 33× drop in energy per Gemini text prompt in a year, while data-centre electricity still rose 17%.3 That is the Jevons shape: cheaper answers invite more of them. The meter’s efficiency and tokens sliders pull opposite ways on purpose.

Electricity is a small share of cost per token today; chips and staff dominate.4 Very cheap tokens are tokens bought in bulk — including by machines.

3Agents asking agents

Human attention caps prompts per person. Growth past that cap needs software that asks on our behalf — or it fails to find a use that pays. The meter lets you set the multiple; it does not pick sides.

For the cheap hours that both data centres and synthetic-fuel plants want to buy, see Cheap demand in the electricity series.

Final words

Demand is the product of people, tasks and tokens. Efficiency can cut the energy of one token and still lose the race if tokens grow faster. Set the ladder and the multiple, then read the verdict meter.
