A square metre of panel in a dawn–dusk orbit sees 1,361 W of sunlight nearly around the clock (short eclipses around the June solstice aside) — the solar constant.1 On the ground the same panel sees night, weather and a lower average. That is the pitch for putting AI in space. This page asks when the pitch survives contact with mass, heat and $/kg to orbit — and shows the ranges, not a single optimistic number.

It is Act 2 of the case. Act 1 asked how big the bill could get on Earth. Here the question is sharper: is orbital compute the only answer to a hundred times more AI? The headline target is 100× by 2035; 1,000× by 2040 is a slider.

1Power needed

Compute = power × efficiency. Headline: 100× today’s AI compute by 2035. With 40%/yr hardware efficiency for a decade → (1.4)10 ≈ 28.9× more compute per watt → need P = 31.2 × 100 / 28.9 ≈ 108 GW (≈3.5× today’s ~31 GW AI data-centre capacity).2 Epoch’s AI data-centre capacity more than doubled in 2025 (13.5 → 31 GW). The grid ceiling default of 316 GW for 2035 assumes that growth slows to 26%/yr (31.2 × 1.2610): my extrapolation, not Epoch’s forecast. Under that ceiling, 100× does not need orbit. But 100× in ten years is only ≈1.6× a year, a strong slowdown too: Epoch measures global AI compute growing about 3.3× a year since 2022, and even 2–2.5× a year makes 2035 10³×–10⁴× today. That is where the claim bites; the today’s trend button sets it.

2Sunlight and heat

Starlink-class arrays: 196 W/m² after cell/temperature/pointing derates (losses from heat, ageing and imperfect sun-pointing; ideal AM0, i.e. sunlight above the atmosphere, at 20% = 272 W/m²).3 One MW of IT → a few thousand m² of PV. In vacuum heat leaves only by radiation: q = ε · F · σ · (T⁴ − Tsink⁴). With emissivity ε=0.9 (how close the coating comes to a perfect radiator), view factor F=0.85 (the share of its view that is cold sky, Turyshev-style) and σ the Stefan–Boltzmann constant, at 320 K against deep space: q ≈ 455 W/m² one-sided → ≈ 1,100 m²/MW two-sided. In low orbit the radiator also soaks up Earth’s infrared and reflected sunlight; lumped into a sink temperature of 200–250 K, that adds roughly 20–60% to the area.4

Radiators are not the cost showstopper (a few percent of system cost in the Forethought read), but they add area to the panels. At 100 GW the radiator is ≈110 km² ideal, ≈130–175 km² with Earth’s glow, about a fifth to a third of the ≈510 km² of panels — large, yet far smaller than the land a terrestrial solar farm would need for the same average power.5

3Mass per kilowatt

The number that decides the economics is how many kilograms of satellite you must launch per kilowatt of IT power. Published claims span a factor of six:

  • SpaceX FCC filing: 10 kg/kW (aspirational)
  • SpaceX Starmind page: 13 kg/kW
  • Suncatcher reference design: about 20 kg/kW
  • Turyshev / JPL bottom-up preprint: 34–59 kg/kW (~40 typical)

The optimistic end are company claims; the pessimistic end is the only independent bottom-up so far. This page’s sliders carry 13 / 25 / 40.6

A hundred gigawatts at 13–40 kg/kW is 1.3–4 million tonnes — about 9,000–27,000 Starship-class launches at 150 tonnes each — against about 145 Starship launches a year in today’s site approvals (~21,750 t/yr: 0.5–1.7 GW/yr across 40–13 kg/kW, ≈0.9 at 25).7 Tonnage, not thermodynamics, is the binding constraint at scale.

4Launch price

Today’s published $/kg disagree by more than 2×: ~$3,600/kg (Suncatcher paper / TechCrunch) vs ~$1,500/kg (Forethought internal-cost basis). Suncatcher’s learning curve reaches $200/kg by ~2035 — a projection, not a contract.8 Launch-only $/kWh = (price/kg) × (kg/kW) ÷ (5 yr × 8,760 h × 0.95). Floor only: excludes satellite build, ops, and chips that fail without repair.

5Only, or not?

At 100× by 2035 the answer is a plain no: the grid alone is enough under Mid assumptions. At 1,000× or 10⁴× the grid runs out and orbit can close a gap — if launches and mass per kilowatt cooperate. “Only” fails when terrestrial supply or demand-side efficiency also close it. The meter above is the argument; this page is the physics behind its sliders.

6Coda: star-mind

Bookend, not a path: Earth intercepts 1.74×10⁵ TW of sunlight; the Sun emits 3.8×10¹⁴ TW.10 At today’s efficiency, 10⁶× today’s AI fleet ≈ 31,000 TW — 18% of sunlight on Earth, ~1,600× human primary power. With 100× better hardware the same compute needs 100× less power. Nothing in Act 1 or Act 2 depends on this.

Honest placement. This is a post-2100 physics bookend, not a 2035–2050 engineering path. Nothing in the Act 1 or Act 2 verdicts depends on it. It adds one message: the long-run limit is thermodynamics (Landauer’s kT ln 2 per bit erased; reversible computing, which erases nothing, could in principle go below it), not the scarcity of joules on Earth.

Final words

Orbit is real as a research programme and unproven on the one number that decides it: kilograms per kilowatt at a launch price that clears the terrestrial band. For 100× by 2035, the grid is enough. For much larger multiples, tonnes and licences decide before physics does. Read the ledger, then mark the tripwires on Act 1.
