Orbital Compute: Is Space the Only Answer?

A square metre of panel in a dawn–dusk orbit sees 1,361 W of sunlight nearly around the clock — the solar constant.1 On the ground the same panel sees night, weather and a lower average. That is the pitch for putting AI in space. This page asks when the pitch survives contact with mass, heat and $/kg to orbit — and shows the ranges, not a single optimistic number.

It is Act 2 of the case. Act 1 asked how big the bill could get on Earth. Here the question is sharper: is orbital compute the only answer to a hundred times more AI? The headline target is 100× by 2035; 1,000× by 2040 is a slider.

1Power needed

Compute = power × efficiency. Headline: 100× today’s AI compute by 2035. With 40%/yr hardware efficiency for a decade → (1.4)10 ≈ 28.9× more compute per watt → need P = 31.2 × 100 / 28.9 ≈ 108 GW (≈3.5× today’s ~31 GW AI data-centre capacity).2 Grid ceiling default 316 GW for 2035 = Epoch’s recent ~26%/yr capacity growth carried forward — my extrapolation, not Epoch’s published forecast. Under that ceiling, 100× does not need orbit. The claim bites at 10³×–10⁴×.

2Sunlight and heat

Starlink-class arrays: 196 W/m² after cell/temperature/pointing derates (ideal AM0 at 20% = 272 W/m²).3 One MW of IT → a few thousand m² of PV. In vacuum heat leaves only by radiation: q = ε · F · σ · T⁴. With ε=0.9, F=0.85 (Turyshev-style), at 320 K: q ≈ 455 W/m² one-sided → ≈ 1,100 m²/MW two-sided.4

Radiators are not the cost showstopper (a few percent of system cost in the Forethought read), but they dominate area alongside the panels. At 100 GW the radiator is on the order of 150–250 km² — large, yet far smaller than the land a terrestrial solar farm would need for the same average power.5

3Mass per kilowatt

The number that decides the economics is how many kilograms of satellite you must launch per kilowatt of IT power. Published claims span a factor of six:

  • SpaceX FCC filing: 10 kg/kW (aspirational)
  • SpaceX Starmind page: 13 kg/kW
  • Suncatcher reference design: about 20 kg/kW
  • Turyshev / JPL bottom-up preprint: 34–59 kg/kW (~40 typical)

The optimistic end are company claims; the pessimistic end is the only independent bottom-up so far. This page’s sliders carry 13 / 25 / 40.6

A hundred gigawatts at 13–40 kg/kW is 1.3–4 million tonnes — about 9,000–27,000 Starship-class launches at 150 tonnes each — against an FAA cap of 145 launches a year (~0.5 GW/yr at the implied mass).7 Tonnage, not thermodynamics, is the binding constraint at scale.

4Launch price

Today’s published $/kg disagree by more than 2×: ~$3,600/kg (Suncatcher paper / TechCrunch) vs ~$1,500/kg (Forethought internal-cost basis). Suncatcher’s learning curve reaches $200/kg by ~2035 — a projection, not a contract.8 Launch-only $/kWh = (price/kg) × (kg/kW) ÷ (5 yr × 8,760 h × 0.95). Floor only: excludes satellite build, ops, and chips that fail without repair.

5Only, or not?

At 100× by 2035 the answer is a plain no: the grid alone is enough under Mid assumptions. At 1,000× or 10⁴× the grid runs out and orbit can close a gap — if launches and mass per kilowatt cooperate. “Only” fails when terrestrial supply or demand-side efficiency also close it. The meter above is the argument; this page is the physics behind its sliders.

6Coda: star-mind

Bookend, not a path: Earth intercepts 1.74×10⁵ TW of sunlight; the Sun emits 3.8×10¹⁴ TW.10 At today’s efficiency, 10⁶× today’s AI fleet ≈ 31,000 TW — 18% of sunlight on Earth, ~1,600× human primary power. With 100× better hardware the same compute needs 100× less power. Nothing in Act 1 or Act 2 depends on this.

Honest placement. This is a post-2100 physics bookend, not a 2035–2050 engineering path. Nothing in the Act 1 or Act 2 verdicts depends on it. It adds one message: the long-run limit is thermodynamics (Landauer’s kT ln 2 per bit erased), not the scarcity of joules on Earth.

Final words

Orbit is real as a research programme and unproven on the one number that decides it: kilograms per kilowatt at a launch price that clears the terrestrial band. For 100× by 2035, the grid is enough. For much larger multiples, tonnes and licences decide before physics does. Read the ledger, then mark the tripwires on Act 1.
