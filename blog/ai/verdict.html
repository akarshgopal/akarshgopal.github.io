1Act 1: the three rungs

Mid is calibrated so inference lands near the IEA’s Base Case path for data centres (~220 TWh of inference in 2030, on my 60% split of the IEA’s ~950 TWh all-DC figure). That calibration is mine, not a forecast. Low and High move the same five levers; the ledger below freezes the three presets from model.js.

The joint solver on the meter reaches R3 only with power built for inference near 3,000 GW and tokens per task above a thousand times today’s — together, not alone.

2Act 2: 100× and beyond

Headline: 100× today’s AI compute by 2035. Derivation: P = 31.2 GW × 100 ÷ (1.4)10 ≈ 108 GW. Grid ceiling 316 GW (2035) is my extrapolation of Epoch’s capacity trend; 600 / 1,500 GW (2040 / 2050) are my assumptions. Under those, 100× needs no orbit. Drag to 10³× or 10⁴× to see where grid then orbit run out.

3Tripwires

Two observables keep the thesis falsifiable without waiting for 2050:

  • Inference ≥ 300 TWh by 2030
  • Inference ≥ 1,000 TWh by 2035

Both are mine. If the path misses them, R1–R3 are effectively dead under this model. Bars: R1 from IEA WEO 2025 (~37,800 TWh in 2035); R2 a cooling-class working bar (IEA commentary 10 Jul 2026); R3 from IEA Future of Heat Pumps (2022) primary PDF. Ongoing: Energy and AI updates, Ember, Energy Institute Statistical Review.

Final words

Under Mid assumptions the answer on Act 1 is NO on every rung, and on Act 2 the grid alone covers 100× by 2035. The interesting region is the joint tail: many levers moved together, or a much larger multiple. Mark the tripwires; return when the next IEA number lands.
